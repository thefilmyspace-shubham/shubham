# Competitive Landscape: Restaurant POS / Restaurant Management Software in India (vs Petpooja), as of late 2026

Research date: 1 Nov 2026. Most "2026" pricing comes from third-party directories (Techjockey, G2, Capterra, SoftwareSuggest) or from blogs published by small competing POS vendors (DineOpen, Restrofi, OrderIt, Forkcast, Knomi, BillFeeds, Chuk, Servyn). Those competitor blogs have an obvious interest in making incumbents look expensive; every figure from them is labelled as such. Almost no Indian vendor publishes an official INR rate card, so the "actual prices" below are triangulated ranges, not list prices. Data older than 2024 is flagged.

## Key Question 1: Competitor-by-competitor profiles (positioning, segment, pricing, features, weaknesses, scale, vs Petpooja)

### Takeaway
India's restaurant-software market has one volume leader (Petpooja, ~1 lakh outlets claimed, FY25 revenue ~₹102 Cr), one enterprise/chain leader (Restroworks, 30,000+ restaurants claimed across 52 countries, quote-only), one commission-model challenger (DotPe/Rista, 55,000+ outlets claimed, FY25 revenue ₹82.8 Cr), one aggregator-integration middleware player that has moved into POS (UrbanPiper, FY25 revenue ₹53.1 Cr, declining, loss-making, backed by Swiggy and Zomato), and a long tail of ₹5,000–₹18,000/year regional POS vendors plus sub-₹1,000/month billing apps. Aggregators themselves (Swiggy, Eternal/Zomato) do not offer a free POS as of late 2026; they expose APIs that third-party POS vendors integrate with.

### Inferences
- Petpooja's ARPU ≈ ₹102 Cr / ~1,00,000 claimed outlets ≈ ₹10,000/outlet/year, which lines up with the ₹10,000–12,000 base-licence figures from directories, suggesting most of its base is on the entry plan and add-on attach is limited.
- UrbanPiper's shrinking revenue and ₹50 Cr loss on ₹53 Cr revenue suggests integration middleware alone is not a sustainable standalone business; its Prime POS at ~₹7,500–10,000/year is priced to match Petpooja's entry tier.
- DotPe's commission model (1–3% of online GMV) and Restroworks' quote-only enterprise model bracket Petpooja's flat ₹10–12k/year from both sides; none of the three publishes a transparent rate card, which is the gap the 2026 micro-entrants (DineOpen ₹899/mo, SlickPOS ₹999/mo, OnlineEmenu ₹199/mo) are attacking.

## Key Question 2: Pricing format of each competitor (with INR prices)

### Takeaway
Four pricing formats coexist: (a) per-outlet annual licence with add-on modules (Petpooja ₹10–12k base, up to ₹30–40k with ops/marketing tiers; Gofrugal ₹9–18k; TMBill ~₹6k; Ciferon ~₹7.5k; Prime POS ~₹7.5–10k); (b) quote-only enterprise with setup fees (Restroworks ₹2.5–12k/outlet/month plus ₹30–80k setup; LimeTray; UrbanPiper Hub/Meraki; Sanguine); (c) commission on online GMV (DotPe 1–3%, free QR tier); (d) cheap monthly SaaS / freemium (SlickPOS ₹999/mo, DineOpen ₹899/mo, OnlineEmenu ₹199/mo, myBillBook ₹199/mo, Zoho POS free–₹649/mo). No Indian vendor bundles hardware the way Toast/Clover do; all recommend commodity Android tablets and 58/80 mm thermal printers.

### Inferences (gaps / opportunities for a new entrant)
- Transparent, all-inclusive pricing (QR + KDS + captain app + aggregator sync in one ₹8–12k/year or ~₹799–999/month plan) directly attacks Petpooja's most-cited complaint (hidden add-on charges) while matching its price floor; the 2026 micro-entrants are doing this but without Swiggy/Zomato/ONDC auto-sync, which is table stakes.
- The 3–10 outlet regional chain is under-served: Petpooja's multi-outlet console is rated "mid-tier" and Restroworks' entry cost (₹30–80k setup, ₹1.5–2.5 lakh/yr) is prohibitive. A product with a strong central console at ~₹15–20k/outlet/year could sit between them.
- Analytics is a differentiator: Petpooja's P&L/analytics are called "basic", UrbanPiper is retreating (revenue down), and only Restroworks offers deep inventory/recipe costing. AI-generated daily P&L, food-cost variance and menu-engineering insight is a plausible wedge, especially as Eternal is already pushing AI into partner apps.
- Direct-channel/ONDC tailwinds (Zomato dropping price parity, Flipkart building on ONDC, 15–30% aggregator commissions) favour a POS that is WhatsApp/ONDC-native out of the box; DotPe owns this positioning but on a commission model that owners dislike at scale, and Petpooja treats direct ordering as a paid add-on.
- Support is the structural weak point of all incumbents; a new entrant must decide between Petpooja-style field support (expensive, loss-making at scale) and remote-only (UrbanPiper's complaint). WhatsApp-based support plus self-serve onboarding is the only cost-viable option for a cheap plan.
- Hardware bundling (Toast/Clover model) is absent in India; all vendors run on commodity Android tablets and 58/80 mm printers, so hardware is not a moat and a new entrant should not build one.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantifies Petpooja's churn, add-on attach rate, or segment mix; inferences above rest on competitor-authored comparisons.
- No evidence that any aggregator (Swiggy/Eternal) plans a free POS; if that changes it would reset the entire low-end market.
- Reddit/Quora/LinkedIn owner sentiment was not retrievable via search results in this session.
